# The Lighthouse on a Planet with No Sea (v2)

_30 beats: 6 authored, 24 bridges (max depth 4)._

## 1. The light at Cape Nothing has burned for 141 years over a sea that never came

_Year 141 of the charter, night of the autumn dust-turn · Cape Nothing lighthouse, rim of the Telemann Basin · author A_

The lighthouse stands on a basalt bluff above the Telemann Basin, a dry bowl of red dust four hundred kilometres wide that the founding charter promised would be filled by the comet-ice project. The ice never came, but the charter was never amended, so the lamp still turns every night. Odile Varr, third keeper of her family, logs the weather in her grandmother's ledger format: wind, visibility, and a column headed SWELL in which every entry for 141 years reads "none."

## 2. Odile lights the lamp at sunset with a petition to put it out on the soup tray

She has written that word more than four thousand times.

Under the ledger is a form that came by the post rover from Telemann town three days ago: _Petition to the Charter Board for Amendment of Article 1 (Maintenance of the Light)_. Odile has filled in every box but one. If the winter session passes it, the lamp at Cape Nothing will be put out for good, and the family will be free to leave the bluff after a hundred and forty-one years. The empty box is the signature of the Principal Keeper of record, and that is not her.

She heats the soup, sets out the bowl, the bread and a soup spoon on the dusk tray, and slides the petition under the bowl. Then she stands in the kitchen for a minute working out how to say it. _Grandmother, the sea is not coming._ She picks up the tray and starts up the stairs to the second landing.

## 3. Ilse Marrow sees a slow star and orders the old watch kept

Low over the basin there is a new star, and it is moving: a warm point that gets brighter and drops lower each minute, like a match lowered toward a table.

"That's a braking flare," Ilse says. "Something big, coming down slowly. Open the marine set."

## 4. The slow star passes under the bluff with a sound like surf, and Ilse asks if it is hunting

_bridge, depth 2_

At 19:44 the descending light, which Odile has logged at bearing 241, is no longer descending. It levels out and slides along the rim toward the bluff, very low, and drops out of sight beneath the watch room's single landward window. A minute later the whole tower hums. Something passes along the foot of Cape Nothing with a long, falling roar. Odile has read the word in her grandmother's books but never heard the sound, and the only word she has for it is _surf_. The windows buzz in their leads. Then it fades out over the basin toward the south-west.

She does not go out to look. Article 9 says _a keeper awake_ beside the set, and the hiss is still running. In the ledger under WIND she writes: _19:45, prolonged roar from basin, no dust raised_. She stops short of writing anything more.

At midnight the speaking tube from the second landing whistles. Ilse's voice comes through it, thin with the brass. "Where is it now?"

"Gone below the rim. It came along the bluff and went back out."

A long pause. "Is it hunting?" Ilse asks. Odile does not know what she means, and her grandmother does not explain. "Keep the set open," Ilse says. "I am taking my drops." ## 6. A mayday arrives on 2182 kHz, a band no one has used for a century

_Year 141, autumn dust-turn, 02:40 · Watch room, Cape Nothing lighthouse · author A_

At 02:40 the old marine set in the watch room, which Odile dusts every week only because the charter requires it, starts to hiss and then speaks. A man's voice, hoarse and very calm, repeats: "Mayday, mayday, this is the coaster Margarethe Holm, taking water forward, request a lee." He gives a latitude and longitude to the second, and when Odile plots it on the charter chart, the position lies eleven kilometres out on the basin floor, in what the chart calls the Outer Roads.

## 7. The plotted mayday lies on bearing 241, and the speaking tube gives back only snoring

_bridge, depth 2_

The second landing is four flights down, and the charter is plain that the watch must be kept _by a keeper awake_ at the set. Odile realises that nobody living has ever answered this frequency from this tower. Ilse never did. Neither did her mother. It will have to be her.

## 17. Four minutes of hover left, and Ilse answers Article 1

## 19. Ilse Marrow turns the lamp down onto the frost

The descending ark _Thalassa's Promise_ runs on a 300-year-old sea-lander autopilot that will only commit to a landing over open water. ## 22. The downwash reaches the pan, the tower shakes, and Ilse holds the spoon in with her bare palm

_bridge, depth 3_

## 25. The ark skids to rest forty metres from the tower

_Same night, 04:31 local, just before dawn · Tethys Pan, at the foot of the lighthouse · author B_
